Earlier this year I emailed a few people a PDF about setting up Claude as a job-search assistant. It was good for about a month. Then the product moved and the PDF stayed where it was, which is the trouble with PDFs. This series replaces it with something I can keep current, and widens the frame past job hunting to whatever you are actually working on. It is written for people I know: accomplished, busy, running a career or a team or a business, who already use Claude a little, or mean to, and have no idea yet what a project, a memory, or a model is. That is fine. Each episode teaches what it needs at the moment it needs it.

## Claude is a thinking partner, not an answer machine

The habit that separates people who get a lot out of Claude from people who get a little is what they bring to it. Most people bring a question. Bring the situation instead: the messy version, with the constraints, the half-formed worry, the thing you are not sure is even the right problem. Claude is good at finding options you had not considered, at telling you when your plan has a hole in it, and at taking the grunt work (the first draft, the summary, the spreadsheet) off your plate so your attention goes where it matters. Treat it like a sharp colleague, not a search box.

## AI is really good at writing prompts, instructions, and configurations for AI

If you take one sentence from this series, take that one: AI is really good at writing prompts, instructions, and configurations for AI. I said it to a friend, a former petroleum engineer who is now a pastor, and watched it unlock the whole thing for him. He had been treating every prompt as something he had to compose from scratch, and the blank page was the barrier.

Here is the consequence. You never have to write a prompt, a set of instructions, or a configuration from nothing. Describe what you want in plain words, ask Claude to write it, then edit what it gives you. Every copy-paste block in this series comes with that fallback: if the block does not fit you, ask Claude for one that does.

## When you are stuck, ask Claude

At any step in any episode, if a menu is not where I said it was, or a button is missing, or something just does not make sense, open a chat and ask. The template is: "I'm trying to do X and I see Y. What do I do?" Claude can look up its own product better than any guide can, and it will walk you the rest of the way.

I mean this literally, with one condition: ask it to search the web. Claude changes faster than anything I can publish, and on its own it does not know about recent changes to its own app. With web search, it can look up Anthropic's current help pages and tell you what they say today. This guide tells you where things were on the day I wrote it; this habit covers where they went. Episode 1 shows how to make sure web search is available.

## How this series was made

I designed it by running a grilling session with Claude: a structured interview in which Claude asks rounds of numbered questions about what I am trying to build, recommends an answer to each, and keeps going until nothing is left assumed. I answered by number, overruled a few recommendations, and came out with a spec that Claude then drafted from. The technique is Matt Pocock's [grilling skill](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-grilling), open-sourced in [his skills repo](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills). Episode 2 gives it the full treatment, including the skill text itself and how to turn it on your own decisions.

Each episode stands on its own, but Episode 1 sets up the account everything else assumes, so start there. The series covers Claude in the browser and the mobile app. There is more beyond that (Claude Desktop and Claude Code), and a later episode covers it. Cowork, which lets Claude take on a multi-step task and carry it out for you instead of answering one prompt at a time, used to be a separate place and is being merged into the regular chat for Pro and Max, so you may already see it there. Episode 3 covers it.
